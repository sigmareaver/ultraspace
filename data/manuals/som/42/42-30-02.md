# 42-30-02 — Elevated Particle Environment (Avionics Protection)

**From:** SEU HAZARD annunciated, or the flux monitor reading above
100 p/cm²·s (`data.seu read`).
**To:** non-essential avionics shed for the duration of the event, DB-A
operating degraded by choice and by record.

WARNING: **This procedure deliberately degrades the data bus.** Shedding a
terminal takes it off the air; DATA BUS A DEGRADED will annunciate and stay
annunciated until the terminal is restored. That lamp is then *expected*, and
a second casualty arriving underneath it will be that much harder to see. Log
the time you shed and the time you restore.

## 1. The trade

An unpowered board cannot latch up (42-00-00 §10). Every terminal you shed is
a terminal the event cannot reach; every terminal you keep is exposed at the
full elevated rate. So the question is never "is it safe" — it is **which
terminals must keep working while this passes**.

On this vessel the answer is fixed by the wiring, not by judgement:

| Terminal | Feed | During an event |
|---|---|---|
| RT 5 | CB E3, BUS E (essential) | **KEEP.** Essential-bus equipment. |
| RT 12 | CB A2, BUS A | **SHED.** Non-essential; this is the one you give up. |
| BC | CB E2, BUS E | **KEEP.** Shed the controller and you are blind, not safe. |

The controller is the instructive case. It is as susceptible as anything else,
and shedding it would protect it — and would also take away the bus table,
the health telemetry and the DEGRADED annunciator, leaving you unable to see
what the event is doing to anything else. Blindness is not protection. Keep
it, and accept the risk with your eyes open.

## 2. Procedure

Execute per checklist `som-42-30-02`:

```
 1. Flux monitor ........................... READ
       above 100 p/cm2s .................... continue
       below ............................... no action; this is not an event
 2. CB A2 (RT 12 feed) ..................... OPEN
       RT 12 is now protected and dark. Note the time.
 3. Wait ................................... 1 s
       three misses before the controller declares it (42-00-00 §3)
 4. DB-A bus table ......................... READ
       expect RT 12 NO RESPONSE, RT 5 OK
       expect DATA BUS A DEGRADED annunciated — this is the cost, not a fault
 5. Flux monitor ........................... MONITOR until below 100 p/cm2s
       DB-A bus table ...................... READ every few minutes
       a kept terminal can fail under the lamp you already lit (§3)
 6. CB A2 .................................. CLOSE
       DB-A health ......................... 1.0 within 3 s
 7. DB-A bus table ......................... READ, expect RT 12 OK
```

Step 6's verification matters more than it looks. A terminal restored after an
event either answers or does not, and if it does not you have learned
something the event could not have told you while the board was dark: the
latch-up happened before you shed it, or the board is failed for an unrelated
reason. Work it from the table (FIM 42-11), not from an assumption about the
weather.

## 3. If the bus degrades anyway

You shed what you could and a terminal you kept has latched up. **Expect no
new annunciation.** DATA BUS A DEGRADED is already lit — you lit it — and it
does not light twice. The second casualty arrives in silence, and the only
places it shows are the bus table and the instruments that terminal carries,
which stop being refreshed and go stale (42-00-00 §8). This is the price of
the trade, and it is why step 5 is a monitoring step and not a wait.
 Nothing here
changes: FIM 42-11 is the tree, power-cycling is the cure, and the board is
a candidate for replacement at the next opportunity. The one thing the event
adds is the entry in the log — **flux at the time of the casualty** — which
is what turns "this board keeps failing" into a maintenance case rather than
a suspicion.
