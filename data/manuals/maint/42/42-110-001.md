# 42-110-001 — Data Bus Remote Terminal — Removal and Installation

**From:** a verdict naming a terminal — FIM 42-11 §4 (latch-up, board
susceptible) or FIM 42-12 §2 (harness innocent, board silent).
**To:** the terminal answering, DB-A health 1.0, the removed unit on the bench.

**Spares:** one 42-110-001 per installation. Confirm it is on the shelf before
you open anything (00-00 §1).

NOTE 1: The whole bus goes down for this, not just the terminal. Removal and
installation are interlocked on the *bus* being de-energized — controller and
every terminal — because a stub is still wired to the trunk whether or not its
box is in the rack. Plan the outage; do not discover it at step 7.

NOTE 2: This task is written for **RT 12** (feed CB A2). For RT 5 the feed is
CB E3 and the address is `data.db.a.rt.5`; nothing else changes.

## Procedure

Execute per checklist `maint-42-110-001`:

```
 1. Terminal records ....................... READ
       part number ........................ 42-110-001
 2. Stores ................................. READ
       spare .............................. ON SHELF
 3. CB E2 (bus controller feed) ............ OPEN
 4. CB A2 (RT 12 feed) ..................... OPEN
 5. CB E3 (RT 5 feed) ...................... OPEN
 6. Allow rails to bleed ................... 1 s
 7. RT 12 module ........................... REMOVE
 8. RT 12 module ........................... INSTALL
 9. CB A2 (RT 12 feed) ..................... CLOSE
10. CB E3 (RT 5 feed) ...................... CLOSE
11. CB E2 (bus controller feed) ............ CLOSE
       DB-A health ........................ 1.0 within 2 s
12. DB-A bus table ......................... READ
       RT 5, RT 12 ........................ OK
```

Steps 9 to 12 are SOM 42-30-01 — terminal feeds before the controller, then the
health and the table — reprinted here because that check *is* the close-out of
this task (00-00 §1). The order matters for the same reason it matters in
checkout: a controller that comes up first declares the dark terminals FAILED,
and you will spend the rest of the flight explaining an error count you caused
yourself.

## Notes

**Step 1 is not a formality.** The records give you the part number to draw and
the serial you are removing. Write the serial down. The bench has no way to tell
your two boards apart later, and neither will you.

**Step 2 is the whole reason this task can fail safely.** If the shelf is empty,
the task refuses here — before the bus is down and before the rack is open. An
empty shelf is not an emergency; a half-finished swap is.

**Between steps 7 and 8 the ship is flying with a hole in it.** That is a
legitimate state and sometimes the intended one: if the removed board is the one
that was babbling, the bus is *healthy without it*, degraded by exactly the
function you removed. Stopping here is a decision, not a mistake — but make it
deliberately, and see 00-00 §3 about what will remind you (nothing).

**The readout says nothing about the old board.** `remove` reports the serial
that came out and not a word about its condition; `install` reports the serial
that went in. Neither tells you whether you guessed right. Step 12 tells you
whether the *function* is back, which is a different and more useful fact: a
terminal that answers after a swap has proved the board was the fault only if
nothing else changed — and you just opened three breakers.

**If the bus does not come back**, you have either replaced an innocent board or
the fault is not in the box. Go to FIM 42-12 with the harness tree, and note in
the log that a module was changed — the next person to work this bus needs to
know the board is a fresh one.
