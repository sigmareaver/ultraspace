# 42-12 — Data Bus Wiring and Medium Faults

**Entry condition:** DATA BUS A DEGRADED annunciated, the bus controller
on line (the table is readable), and either:

- **one terminal dark with its feed known good** (FIM 42-11 §2 sent you), or
- **every terminal dark after FIM 42-11's cycling restored nothing** — the
  babbling-terminal theory is dead; the medium is the suspect.

Have WDM 42 open. You will be pointing at places in it.

## 1. The doctrine, once

The harness is physical (42-00-00 §2). A break partitions the bus and the
dark terminals are *contiguous from the far end*. A trunk short kills
everything, and a shorted coupler is a trunk short. A stub fault touches
only its terminal — transformer coupling guarantees it. The DMM is the
arbiter, and it only speaks to a dead bus.

NOTE: **De-energize DB-A before any ohms check** — open CB E2, CB E3,
CB A2 — and allow one second for the rails to bleed. An open breaker is
not a dead rail, and the meter refuses a live bus.

Read the trunk and you read the map: 78 ohm is a terminator seen through
unbroken cable, OL is a break in that direction, ~0 ohm is a short. A
stub tap reads its coupling winding (~2 ohm) when the run is whole.

## 2. One terminal dark, feed good

The candidate set is the stub, the coupler, the board, or the trunk
feeding it. Work the terminal's own coupler (RT 12: J2; RT 5: J1):

```
 1. De-energize DB-A (NOTE above).
 2. DMM the stub tap:
       OL ................ stub open → replace the stub run
       ~0 ohm ............ stub short → replace the stub run
       ~2 ohm ............ stub is innocent → step 3
 3. DMM the trunk toward the BC (RT 12's case: seg.j1-j2 at J2):
       OL ................ break between the couplers → replace the segment
       78 ohm ............ this coupler's own face is sound → step 4
 4. DMM the SAME run from the coupler upstream (at J1, toward J2):
       OL ................ the run is sound from the far end but broken from
                          here: the break is inside the coupler you were
                          standing on → replace the coupler
       78 ohm ............ harness innocent end to end → the board is dead →
                          replace the module (IPC 42-110-001)
```

RT 5 has no step 3: it taps J1, and J1 is fed directly by seg.bc-j1. A
fault in that run, or in J1 itself, takes the whole bus down — so it
cannot be what darkened one terminal. Stub, then board.

WARNING: **A coupler cannot show you its own break.** An open feed-through
separates the coupler's two trunk faces, and each face still reads its own
way out — 78 ohm, innocent-looking, from the probe point standing on the
fault. Confirm a coupler from the *next coupler down*. A shorted coupler is
the opposite: it is across the pair right where you are standing, so it
reads ~0 in both directions at its own probe point.

## 3. Every terminal dark (the trunk tree)

FIM 42-11 already cleared the babbling terminal. What remains is the
medium: a short anywhere on the trunk, or a break upstream of everything.

```
 1. De-energize DB-A (NOTE above).
 2. DMM at J1, toward the BC:
       OL ................ break in seg.bc-j1 → replace it
       ~0 ohm ............ a short, but whose? → step 3
       78 ohm ............ → step 4
 3. DMM at J1, toward J2 (you read ~0 toward the BC):
       ~0 ohm ............ ~0 BOTH ways at one probe point: the short is
                          across the pair right there → replace coupler J1
       78 ohm ............ one way only → short in seg.bc-j1 → replace it
 4. DMM at J1, toward J2 (you read 78 ohm toward the BC):
       ~0 ohm ............ the short is in the J1-J2 interval → step 5
       78 ohm ............ trunk clean both ways at J1 → step 6
 5. The interval holds two suspects — the run and the coupler at its far
    end — and ohms cannot separate them: a short in either puts ~0 at both
    ends of the run. Work it in cost order:
       replace seg.j1-j2 → re-energize → verify (§4)
       bus whole ......... the run was the short. Done.
       still dead ........ de-energize again → replace coupler J2
 6. DMM at J2, toward J1:
       OL ................ the run is sound from J1 but open from J2: the
                          break is inside J1 → replace coupler J1
       78 ohm ............ harness innocent — the controller is the suspect
                          (FIM 42-13, pending)
```

A break in a *run* is found from either side: OL looking at it no matter
which coupler you stand at. A break in a *coupler* is found from one side
only — the other. That asymmetry is not a quirk of the meter; it is what
tells the two apart.

## 4. After the replacement

A part comes out and a part goes in; the meter does not tell you whether
you guessed right, and neither does the readout. Re-energize in checkout
order (SOM 42-30-01: terminal feeds first, then the controller) and verify
health 1.0. The error counters will still show the episode — that is the
evidence trail, leave it. If the bus does not come back, either your guess
was wrong (§3 step 5 tells you what to take next) or a second fault is
present: restart from the table (FIM 42-11) with the first one logged.

Execute the whole tree per checklist `fim-42-12`. The checklist is the
contract: it was run against every fault it names before it reached your
hands.
