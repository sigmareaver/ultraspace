# 42-11 — Data Bus Degraded

**Entry condition:** DATA BUS A DEGRADED annunciated, and the bus controller
is on line (the bus table is readable). If the table reads NO DATA, the
controller is the first suspect, not the bus: verify CB E2 and BUS E
voltage before any of the following (42-00-00 §5). Do not troubleshoot a
bus you cannot see.

## 1. Split on the table

Read the bus table (`data.db.a read`) and split on the pattern:

- **One terminal NO RESPONSE, the rest OK** → §2.
- **Every terminal NO RESPONSE** → §3.

## 2. One terminal dark

The suspect is that terminal or its feed, in that order of cheapness:

1. Check the terminal's feed breaker. An open breaker is the answer; close
   it, watch the terminal return, and ask yourself why it was open.
2. Feed good (breaker closed, bus voltage present)? The terminal's board is
   the suspect. Cycle the feed once; a terminal that recovers and stays
   recovered had a transient, not a fault. Log it and move on.
3. A terminal that will not answer with a known-good feed is a board
   replacement (IPC 42-110-001) — FIM 42-12, *pending*.

## 3. Every terminal dark (the jam)

One bad terminal can drag the whole bus down. A transceiver latched
*stuck-dominant* babbles continuously; while it holds, no traffic passes
for anyone. This is the pattern: all terminals missing, declared within
tenths of a second of each other.

A latch-up is a parasitic conduction path. It holds for as long as the
board is powered and it clears — completely, every time — when power is
removed. That gives you the test and the temporary cure in one action:

**Cycle each terminal feed in turn: OPEN, hold 1 second, CLOSE. The bus
recovers the moment the babbling terminal goes dark. That terminal is the
verdict.**

Execute per checklist `fim-42-11`:

```
 1. Bus table ...................... every terminal NO RESPONSE?
 2. CB A2 (RT 12 feed) ............. OPEN, hold 1 s, CLOSE
        bus recovers ............... verdict: RT 12
 3. CB E3 (RT 5 feed) .............. OPEN, hold 1 s, CLOSE
        bus recovers ............... verdict: RT 5
```

The verdict is the terminal whose cycling restored the bus — not the first
suspicious thing you saw. Work the feeds in order and stop when it
recovers.

If the bus does not recover after every terminal feed has been cycled, the
suspect is the controller or the medium itself (FIM 42-12, *pending*).

## 4. After the verdict

Service is restored; the fault is not fixed. The latch-up cleared because
the board lost power, but whatever flipped the bit — a single-event upset
— is still out there, and the board has now shown you it is susceptible.

- Expect recurrence. It may be minutes, months, or never.
- Schedule board replacement at the next maintenance opportunity
  (IPC 42-110-001; swap procedure: MAINT, *pending*).
- If the slot cannot be spared, deferral with the terminal left dark is a
  formal option with the controller's remaining terminals unaffected —
  MEL 42-01, *pending*.

Record the episode in the log: which terminal, when, and that power-
cycling cleared it. The error counters on the bus table carry the same
story; the FDR carries the rest.
