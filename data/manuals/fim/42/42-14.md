# 42-14 — Instrument Reading Frozen

**Entry condition:** an indication has stopped changing, or is marked stale —
`?` on the panel, `? STALE` in the printed line, with the age climbing beside
it. There may be no annunciator at all. This section does not repair anything;
it decides *which system you are actually working on*, and hands you to the one
that does.

## 1. Why this section exists

A frozen indication has two completely different causes and they demand
opposite responses:

- The **reporting chain** failed — the terminal carrying that sensor stopped
  answering the controller. The quantity is unchanged; only your view of it
  is gone. Work the data system.
- The **measured system** failed, and the indication is frozen because the
  hardware that reports it went down with it. Work that system.

They look identical on the gauge. The second case is the dangerous one in the
opposite direction too: an indication frozen at a *healthy-looking* value
while the bus behind it is dead reads like good news for as long as you let
it. Do not reason from the frozen number. Reason from the instruments that
cannot freeze.

## 2. The rule

**Panel-wired indications cannot go stale.** Bus voltages, battery volts and
state of charge, breaker and contactor positions are hard-wired to their
indications (42-00-00 §8) — they are live or the ship is dark, with nothing in
between. Only the carried readings can freeze:

| Reading | Carried by | Feed behind the carrier |
|---|---|---|
| `eps.load.cabin` | RT 12 | CB A2, BUS A |
| `eps.load.avionics` | RT 5 | CB E3, BUS E |

So prove the measured system with the panel-wired instruments *before* you
touch the data network. Two cheap readings do it: the bus the load sits on,
and the load's own breaker.

NOTE: The caution system obeys the same limit you do. A monitor whose source
has gone stale drops quiet and releases any caution it was holding
(42-00-00 §8). During a data casualty **the absence of a caution proves
nothing** — which is exactly why a frozen gauge, and not a lamp, is the entry
symptom here.

## 3. The tree

```
 1. The stale indication ........... which one? (cabin → §3a, avionics → §3b)
        neither stale ............. entry symptom absent. A reading that
                                    changed, changed for real — work the
                                    system it names (ATA 24).
 2. Its load's BUS voltage ......... panel-wired, cannot be stale
        low / dead ................ the BUS is down. Electrical casualty,
                                    not data. The frozen gauge is a second
                                    symptom of it (ATA 24). STOP HERE.
        normal .................... the load is being fed → step 3
 3. Its load's breaker ............. panel observation, cannot be stale
        OPEN ...................... the load really is off. Not a data
                                    fault; ask why the breaker is open.
        CLOSED .................... the load is drawing → step 4
 4. Bus table (data.db.a read)
        NO DATA ................... VERDICT: the controller is unpowered.
                                    EVERY carried indication is stale and
                                    DATA BUS A DEGRADED cannot fire
                                    (42-00-00 §6). CB E2, BUS E voltage.
        carrier NO RESPONSE ....... VERDICT: the carrier terminal is dark.
                                    This is a data casualty — work it at
                                    FIM 42-11 §1 and the indication returns
                                    with the terminal.
        bus HEALTHY, carrier OK ... the transport is intact and the
                                    indication is still frozen: the
                                    transducer or its wiring to the
                                    terminal is the suspect (MAINT,
                                    *pending*).
```

§3a works the cabin chain (BUS A, CB A1, RT 12); §3b works the avionics chain
(BUS E, CB E1, RT 5). The steps are the same; only the hardware changes.

## 4. After the verdict

Nothing here restores an indication — the section that owns the fault does
that, and the reading comes back with it. Two things are worth doing before
you leave:

- **Note the age when you found it.** How long the chain had been silent is
  the only record of how long you were flying on a number that was not being
  refreshed.
- **Re-read the indication after the repair and watch it move.** A value that
  returns and then sits still is not the same as a value that returns. The
  age tag is the difference, and it is the only proof you get.

Execute the whole tree per checklist `fim-42-14`. The checklist is the
contract: it was run against every entry case it names — including the two
that leave the data system entirely — before it reached your hands.
