# 00-00 — Using this book

This is the **Quick Reference Handbook**. It answers one question: *what do I
do in the next sixty seconds?* It does not answer *what is broken* — that is
the FIM — and it does not answer *how is this ship operated* — that is the SOM.
Every checklist in here ends by handing you to one of those two, or by telling
you to monitor and carry on.

Read this page now, while nothing is wrong. You will not have time later.

## 1. The panel tells you what, and in what order

The annunciator panel ranks everything it says. Read it with
`sys.annunciator read`, and **work it from the top**:

```
 ! ▲ WARNING   DATA BUS A FAILED        <- work this
   ▲ CAUTION   DATA BUS A DEGRADED
   ● ADVISORY  ...
```

- **`▲ WARNING`** — red. The ship or the mission is in immediate trouble. Work
  it now, from this book.
- **`▲ CAUTION`** — amber. Real, abnormal, and you have time to think. Work it
  from this book before it compounds into a warning.
- **`● ADVISORY`** — cyan. Know it. Do nothing about it.
- **`!`** — this lamp is **NEW**: it was raised and nobody has acknowledged it.

Within a severity, new lamps come first. That is the only recency the panel
gives you, and it is deliberate: **the newest problem is very often a
consequence of the worst one**, and a book that sent you chasing consequences
would be worse than no book.

## 2. Acknowledge early. It is not a dismissal

`sys.annunciator ack` acknowledges every lit lamp. It does **not** clear them —
a lamp stays lit for as long as its condition holds, and acknowledging says
only *I have seen this*.

Acknowledge as your first action, always, and understand why: an acknowledged
panel is the only panel that can tell you about the **next** thing. The master
lights flash for anything new and go steady once you have seen it, so a panel
full of unacknowledged lamps is a panel that has stopped being able to get
your attention.

The log records that you were told and that you answered. A warning that was
raised and never acknowledged is a finding about the crew.

## 3. A lamp that is already lit will not light again

This is the trap this book exists to keep you out of.

If you have *deliberately* degraded something — shed a terminal, isolated a
bus, pulled a feed — the lamp that reports it is now lit for a reason you
chose, and it will stay lit. **A second, real casualty underneath it announces
itself only if it crosses a different threshold.** That is why the panel
carries both `DATA BUS A DEGRADED` (caution: something is missing) and
`DATA BUS A FAILED` (warning: all of it is missing), and why any checklist
that tells you to hold a degraded configuration also tells you to keep
reading the system's own display while you hold.

When you configure the ship into an annunciated state on purpose, say so out
loud, note the time, and treat every later reading of that system as suspect
until you have looked at it directly.

## 4. Lamp test

`sys.annunciator test` lights every lamp for two seconds. Do it at the start of
every shift and in the cold & dark checklist. A lamp that does not light during
a test cannot warn you, and you will not discover that during the casualty it
was supposed to announce.
